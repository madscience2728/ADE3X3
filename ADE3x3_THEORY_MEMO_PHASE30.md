## 1. Statement Of Theorem Ladder

The proof program consists of three theorems and a conclusion, with strict logical dependence A → B ← C → Conclusion.

**Theorem A (Delta-Containment).** For every exact minimum-rank R decomposition of the 3×3 matrix multiplication tensor into R rank-1 terms (α_k, β_k, γ_k), the dead-X column space satisfies

> col(Δ) ⊂ span(H)

where H = [Η₁ | Η₂] is the R × 18 anisotropy block and Δ is the R × 54 dead-X block, all defined in the fiber-mode basis of Step 51.

**Theorem B (Conservation Law).** Assuming Theorem A and the already-proved fiber-mode faithfulness (Part 1, Phase 17), every minimum-rank 3×3 decomposition satisfies

> R + η_nullity = 27

where η_nullity = 18 − rank(H).

*Logical chain:* Part 1 gives rank([Σ | H | Δ]) = R. The constraint Γ·Σ = 3I₉ forces rank(Γ) = 9, so dim(ker Γ) = R − 9. Since Γ annihilates both H and Δ, we have col([H | Δ]) ⊆ ker(Γ) and, by Part 1, col([H | Δ]) = ker(Γ) with rank R − 9. Theorem A then gives col(H) = ker(Γ), so rank(H) = R − 9, hence η_nullity = 18 − (R − 9) = 27 − R. The conservation law follows.

**Theorem C (η-Nullity Bound).** For every minimum-rank 3×3 decomposition,

> η_nullity ≤ 4

or equivalently rank(H) ≥ R − 9 − (some small deficiency).

**Conclusion.** From B and C: R = 27 − η_nullity ≥ 27 − 4 = 23.

This is a structural lower bound on the bilinear complexity of 3×3 matrix multiplication.

**Dependency graph:**
- Theorem B requires: Theorem A + Part 1 (proved).
- Conclusion requires: Theorem B + Theorem C.
- Theorems A and C are logically independent of each other.

## 2. Canonical Exact Definitions

All notation follows the canon (Steps 51–52, Phase 17).

**Fiber-mode decomposition.** Given an R-term bilinear algorithm with factors (α_k, β_k, γ_k), define for each term k and fiber (r, u):

- σ_k[r,u] = Σ_s α_k[r,s] · β_k[s,u]  (fiber sum, 9 coordinates)
- η1_k[r,u], η2_k[r,u]  (live-anisotropy, 9+9 = 18 coordinates)
- δ_k[r,s,t,u] for s ≠ t  (dead-X, 54 coordinates)

Assembling these row-wise gives the matrices:

| Matrix | Shape | Role |
|--------|-------|------|
| Γ | 9 × R | Output weights γ_k[r',u'] |
| Σ | R × 9 | Fiber-sum coordinates |
| H = [Η₁ \| Η₂] | R × 18 | Live-anisotropy coordinates |
| Δ | R × 54 | Dead-X coordinates |

**Exact tensor system:**

- Γ · Σ = 3 I₉  (81 fiber-sum equations)
- Γ · Η₁ = 0  (81 live-anisotropy equations, first block)
- Γ · Η₂ = 0  (81 live-anisotropy equations, second block)
- Γ · Δ = 0  (486 dead-X equations)

**Per-channel right-inverse structure.** For 3×3 multiplication, there are three summation channels s = 0, 1, 2, each giving a live-product matrix P_s (R × 9) satisfying Γ · P_s = I₉. Setting X₀ = (P₀ + P₁ + P₂)/3 and K_s = P_s − X₀ recovers:

- Η₁ = K₀ − K₁
- Η₂ = K₁ − K₂
- K₀ + K₁ + K₂ = 0

So H is generated by the difference sector of three right inverses of Γ.

**η-nullity.** Defined as η_nullity = 18 − rank(H). Equivalently, it counts the dimension of the left null space of H restricted to the anisotropy coordinates.

**Delta-containment equivalences (from the proof skeleton).** Given Part 1, the following are equivalent:
1. col(Δ) ⊂ span(H)
2. col(H) = ker(Γ)
3. rank(H) = R − 9
4. R + η_nullity = 27

**Nuisance span.** N = col([Η₁ | Η₂ | Δ]) is the full nuisance span. The per-algorithm quotient bound is R ≥ 9 + rank(N).

## 3. Gauge Issues

Several quantities in this framework are gauge-dependent (they change under admissible reparameterizations) while others are gauge-invariant.

**Gauge-invariant (intrinsic to the decomposition):**
- R (the number of rank-1 terms)
- rank(Γ) = 9
- dim(ker Γ) = R − 9
- rank([Σ | H | Δ]) = R  (fiber-mode faithfulness, proved)
- rank([H | Δ])  (equals R − 9 by faithfulness)
- Whether col(Δ) ⊂ span(H)  (this is a property of the column spaces, not the bases)
- rank(H), rank(Δ), rank(N)
- η_nullity = 18 − rank(H)
- The conservation sum R + η_nullity (if well-defined)
- κ_ker(x) = σ_min(Q_x^T H_x)  (honest kernel-restricted defect)

**Gauge-dependent (change under term relabeling, rescaling, or basis changes):**
- Individual entries of Σ, H, Δ
- The specific matrix M in Δ = H · M (the projection coefficients)
- The specific right inverses P_s and lifts K_s (these depend on the choice of Γ's right inverse)
- The forcing direction λ in the continuation phases

**Key observation:** The Delta-containment statement col(Δ) ⊂ span(H) is gauge-invariant: it depends only on column spaces, not on any particular matrix representation. Therefore Theorem A, if true, is a genuine structural property of the decomposition, not an artifact of a coordinate choice.

**The centered-lift picture** (K₀, K₁, K₂ with K₀ + K₁ + K₂ = 0) is also gauge-invariant in the following sense: the span of {K₀, K₁, K₂} is determined by the decomposition, even though the individual K_s depend on the choice of X₀. The lift-span rank (= 2 for both known valid decompositions) is invariant.

## 4. Candidate Proof Route A: Algebraic-Geometric Codimension

**Idea.** Show that the locus of R-term factorized bilinear algorithms where col(Δ) ⊄ span(H) has codimension too high to intersect the exact multiplication variety.

**Formulation.** The parameter space is α_k ∈ ℝ^{3×3}, β_k ∈ ℝ^{3×3}, γ_k ∈ ℝ^{3×3} for k = 1, …, R. The exact multiplication variety V_R is cut out by 729 polynomial equations. The "Delta-leakage" locus L_R is the set of factorized (α, β, γ) where rank([H | Δ]) > rank(H), or equivalently, some Δ column lies outside span(H).

**Needed lemmas:**
1. *Dimension of V_R:* Compute or bound dim(V_R) as a real algebraic variety. The expected dimension comes from 3R · 27 parameters minus 729 equations minus the GL(R) gauge redundancy.
2. *Codimension of L_R inside the full parameter space:* Determine the codimension of the set of factorized triples where the anisotropy block fails to span ker(Γ).
3. *Transversality:* Show V_R ∩ L_R = ∅, or at least that codim(L_R) > dim(V_R).

**Obstruction.** This route can at best show that failure of Theorem A is non-generic. It cannot rule out exceptional valid decompositions on lower-dimensional components of V_R. The Phase 17 canon explicitly warns: "Genericity can only show that failure of rank(H) = R − 9 is lower-dimensional; it cannot rule out exceptional valid decompositions."

**Failure mode.** If the multiplication variety has multiple irreducible components (as is likely for R = 23), some component could lie entirely inside L_R. Genericity arguments are blind to this. Additionally, the Phase 18 data shows that synthetic random right-inverse triples always saturate ker(Γ), but this is the affine right-inverse model, not the factorized model. The factorized model adds polynomial constraints that could create exceptional behavior.

**Verdict:** Insufficient alone. Could serve as supporting evidence that the statement is "morally true," but cannot close the proof.

## 5. Candidate Proof Route B: Per-Channel Kernel Saturation

**Idea.** Exploit the specific algebraic structure of 3×3 matrix multiplication — the three summation channels and the per-channel identity Γ · P_s = I₉ — to show that the difference vectors K₀ − K₁ and K₁ − K₂ must span all of ker(Γ).

**Formulation.** Each P_s is not a generic right inverse of Γ. It has the factorized form P_s[k, (r,u)] = α_k[r,s] · β_k[s,u], so P_s is a rank-at-most-R matrix whose rows are outer products of specific columns and rows of the α and β factors. The key algebraic constraint is:

> H = [K₀ − K₁ | K₁ − K₂] spans ker(Γ)

which, by K₀ + K₁ + K₂ = 0, is equivalent to: the K_s have lift-span rank 2, and their column spans jointly fill ker(Γ).

**Needed lemmas:**
1. *Factorized diversity:* In any minimum-rank decomposition, the three P_s matrices must be "sufficiently different" from each other. The R = 9 impossibility theorem (N1–N8 chain) already shows that all nuisance must vanish at R = 9, causing a contradiction. Extend this pressure argument upward.
2. *Channel-separation from multiplication constraints:* Show that the 729 tensor equations force the three summation channels to contribute algebraically independent kernel directions. Specifically, the equation Γ · Σ = 3I₉ combined with Γ · (P_s − P_t) = 0 for s ≠ t must force enough independence in {K_s − K_t}.
3. *Rank of the difference sector:* Prove that for factorized P_s from a valid multiplication decomposition, rank([K₀ − K₁ | K₁ − K₂]) = R − 9.

**Obstruction.** The Phase 19 data shows that pair-equality (K₀ = K₁) is strongly incompatible with exact multiplication in factorized families, while collinearity of the K_s is too weak (it contains the standard algorithm). The obstruction therefore lies between these: not literal equality, but also not mere collinearity. The exact algebraic condition that intermediate failure modes are incompatible with multiplication is the hard part.

**Failure mode.** Phase 20 already rules out the simplest codimension-one defect (a single extra scalar relation among K₀, K₁, K₂). Phase 24 shows there is no universal first-order obstruction. So any proof along this route must work at the nonlinear level or use global algebraic structure rather than perturbative arguments.

**Verdict:** This is the most structurally motivated route. The per-channel identity is the sharpest algebraic feature distinguishing exact multiplication from generic rank-R collections. But turning the empirical incompatibility of defective loci into a theorem requires either a clever algebraic identity or a nonlinear global argument. This is the recommended primary attack direction.

## 6. Candidate Proof Route C: Nuisance Budget / Dimensional Pressure

**Idea.** Instead of proving Δ ⊂ span(H) directly, prove a strong enough universal lower bound on rank(H) that the conservation law follows by dimension counting.

**Formulation.** From the nuisance budget table (Step 61):
- At rank R, dim(ker Γ) = R − 9.
- The nuisance block N = [H | Δ] maps R rows into 72 columns (18 anisotropy + 54 dead-X).
- rank(N) ≤ R − 9 (since Γ annihilates all nuisance columns).
- The dead-free classification (D1–D5) shows that any term with nonzero dead-X coordinates must have multi-channel support.

**Strategy:** Show that the 54 dead-X coordinates cannot "use up" extra kernel dimensions beyond what H already spans. Formally: rank([H | Δ]) − rank(H) = 0, which is exactly Theorem A restated.

**Alternative weaker form:** Show rank(H) ≥ R − 9 − c for some small constant c. If c = 0 this is Theorem A; if c > 0 it is a weaker statement that may still be provable and could combine with an independent η_nullity bound.

**Needed lemmas:**
1. *Dead-X dependency on anisotropy:* For each term k, the 54-dimensional dead-X row δ_k is determined by the α_k, β_k factors. The anisotropy rows η1_k, η2_k are also determined by the same factors. Show that, at the level of column spaces over all R terms, the δ columns are algebraic combinations of the η columns.
2. *Single-term cross-ratio propagation:* Phase 17 identifies 324 independent cross-ratio identities from the single-term fiber structure, but notes that "linear propagation from live slices to dead slices = False." This is the core difficulty.

**Obstruction.** The single-term identities do not linearly propagate from live coordinates to dead coordinates. The Δ ⊂ span(H) property must therefore be a collective (multi-term) property that depends on the global structure of the decomposition, not a per-term identity.

**Failure mode.** If the dead-X columns can be shown to lie in span(H) only by using relations that couple multiple terms through the γ weights, then the proof must involve Γ itself. But Γ · Δ = 0 and Γ · H = 0 both hold, so Γ gives no *direct* leverage to relate Δ and H. The leverage must come from the factorized structure α_k[r,s] · β_k[t,u] that ties the coordinates together term-by-term.

**Verdict:** This route is equivalent to Route B in disguise — it leads back to the same hard problem. But the dimensional-pressure framing is useful for bookkeeping and for identifying what a "partial Theorem A" would look like.

## 7. Weakest Useful Theorem

If Theorem A as stated (col(Δ) ⊂ span(H) universally) is too strong, the weakest modification that still implies the conservation law Theorem B is:

**Theorem A' (Kernel Saturation).** For every minimum-rank 3×3 decomposition, rank(H) = R − 9.

This is logically equivalent to Theorem A given Part 1 (proved), so there is no weaker replacement that still gives the full conservation law. They are the same statement.

However, there are useful weaker targets that give partial results:

**Theorem A'' (Bounded Leakage).** For every minimum-rank 3×3 decomposition,

> rank([H | Δ]) − rank(H) ≤ c

for some explicit constant c ≥ 0. Combined with Part 1 (rank([H | Δ]) = R − 9), this gives rank(H) ≥ R − 9 − c, hence η_nullity ≤ 18 − (R − 9 − c) = 27 − R + c, hence R ≥ 27 − η_nullity + c. For this to be useful as a lower bound on R, one also needs an upper bound on η_nullity.

**Theorem A''' (Generic Conservation).** For every minimum-rank 3×3 decomposition that is a smooth point of the multiplication variety, col(Δ) ⊂ span(H).

This would still leave open the question of singular decompositions, but would cover all non-degenerate algorithms. Route A (codimension) could potentially prove this version.

**Recommendation:** Pursue Theorem A (= A') as stated. The equivalence of the four formulations in Section 2 means there is no logical room for weakening; either you prove kernel saturation or you don't get the conservation law. But Theorem A'' with explicit c > 0 would still be a publishable structural result, especially if c is small enough that combined with Theorem C it gives R ≥ 22 or better.

## 8. Assessment Of Eta-Nullity Bound

**Current data:**
- AlphaTensor rank-23: η_nullity = 4
- Standard rank-27: η_nullity = 0
- No other exact 3×3 decompositions have been profiled in the canon

**Is Theorem C independently attackable?**

Partially yes. The η-nullity measures 18 − rank(H), which is 18 minus the rank of the R × 18 anisotropy block. For any R-term decomposition where at least R − 9 of the 18 anisotropy columns are linearly independent (as term-space vectors), we get η_nullity ≤ 18 − (R − 9) = 27 − R. For R = 23, this gives η_nullity ≤ 4, which is exactly the observed value.

But this is circular: it assumes rank(H) = R − 9, which is Theorem A. Without Theorem A, the bound η_nullity ≤ 18 is trivial (H has only 18 columns).

**Independent approach.** The anisotropy block H = [Η₁ | Η₂] has columns that are differences of factorized terms. The η-nullity counts how many anisotropy directions are "wasted" (don't contribute new dimensions). A direct bound would require showing that the 18 anisotropy coordinates of R terms cannot all collapse to a low-dimensional subspace without contradicting the multiplication equations.

The dead-free term classification (D1–D5) provides a structural anchor: a dead-free term at channel s contributes exactly one dimension of anisotropy (via the embeddings E₀, E₁, E₂). So if many terms are dead-free, the anisotropy rank is forced upward. But the R = 9 chain shows R < 27 requires nonzero dead-X coordinates, so not all terms can be dead-free. The balance between dead-free and non-dead-free terms is itself constrained by the multiplication equations.

**Assessment:** Theorem C is NOT cleanly attackable independently of Theorem A in its full strength. The natural approaches lead back to the same factorized structure questions. However, a *conditional* version — "assuming Theorem A, η_nullity ≤ 4" — is trivially true by dimension counting for R ≥ 23.

The more productive approach is to prove Theorem A and get Theorem C for free via the conservation law, rather than trying to prove Theorem C first.

## 9. Recommended Next Derivation Step

Based on this assessment, the recommended priority ordering is:

### Priority 1: Formalize the per-channel factorized constraint

The sharpest structural feature is that P_s[k, (r,u)] = α_k[r,s] · β_k[s,u] is an outer product. This means each column of P_s (indexed by the 9 fiber coordinates (r,u)) is a product of a column of α and a row of β restricted to channel s. The difference K₀ − K₁ therefore has the form:

> (K₀ − K₁)[k, (r,u)] = α_k[r,0]·β_k[0,u] − α_k[r,1]·β_k[1,u]

This is a rank-2 matrix in the (r, u) indices for each term k. The question reduces to: can R such rank-2 perturbations, summing to annihilation by Γ, fail to span ker(Γ)?

**Concrete next step:** Write down the exact algebraic conditions on (α, β) under which the R × 9 matrix K₀ − K₁ has column rank strictly less than dim(ker Γ) − rank(K₁ − K₂). Show this locus is incompatible with Γ · P_s = I₉.

### Priority 2: Exploit the R = 9 impossibility upward

The R = 9 theorem chain is the one fully proved structural result. It works by showing that at R = 9, ker(Γ) is trivial, so all nuisance must vanish per-term, which contradicts the dead-free classification. The quantitative version of this pressure at R = 23 is that ker(Γ) has dimension 14, the nuisance block has 72 columns, and the multiplication equations impose at least 72 − 14 = 58 linear dependencies among nuisance columns. Making those 58 dependencies explicit and showing they force col(Δ) into span(H) is a concrete algebraic program.

### Priority 3: Study the standard algorithm as a test case

The standard algorithm (R = 27) has H saturating ker(Γ) with η_nullity = 0 and all terms dead-free. It is the unique case where the conservation law is trivially saturated. Understanding *why* it works — i.e., what structural features of the standard algorithm force kernel saturation — and then identifying which of those features persist for R < 27 would give a template for the general proof.

### What NOT to pursue next

- Do not pursue continuation/homotopy obstruction (Phases 21–30). This line has been thoroughly explored and shows that first-order methods are insufficient and nonlinear persistence is the actual barrier. The continuation approach is an empirical diagnostic, not a proof path.
- Do not pursue numerical/computational verification on additional decompositions. The current evidence base (AlphaTensor + 23 deletions + 1000 random negatives) is strong enough. What is needed is algebraic structure, not more data points.
- Do not attempt to prove Theorem C independently before Theorem A. As argued in Section 8, Theorem C is a corollary of Theorem A, not an independent theorem.

### Summary of open problem

The single hardest step in the entire program is:

> **Show that factorized outer-product structure of the per-channel matrices P_s, combined with the identity Γ · P_s = I₉ and minimum-rank, forces the difference sector span{K₀ − K₁, K₁ − K₂} to saturate ker(Γ).**

This is a polynomial algebra problem on the variety of minimum-rank factorized bilinear algorithms for 3×3 matrix multiplication. It is the remaining gap between the proved Part 1 and the target conservation law.
